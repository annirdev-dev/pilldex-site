Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 18, 2026

This policy is for residents of Washington (My Health My Data Act), Nevada (SB 370), Connecticut and other states with consumer health data laws. It adds to our Privacy Policy.

In short

  • As a guest, your medicines, doses, counts, pill photos and the pills you look up are stored only on your phone. We don’t collect them.
  • If you choose to sign in, we collect your people, medicines, doses and counts, only after you agree, to back them up and sync your phones.
  • We never sell consumer health data and never share it for advertising.
  • The app doesn’t use your location, so it can’t geofence health care facilities.

Consumer health data we collect, and why

DataWhyWhere it comes from
Technical request logs when the app downloads an official pill photo: IP address, request details, and the photo’s file name (which identifies a pill) To deliver the photo, keep the service secure, and fix technical problems Your device, automatically, when a pill photo is shown
If you sign in: the names of the people you add, their medicines (name, strength, schedule, dates, pills left, the pill’s look), the dose log and pill counts To back up your data and keep your phones in sync You, after you agree on the sign-in screen
Anything health-related you choose to write in an email to us To answer your question You

As a guest, everything you enter or create in the app stays on your device and is not collected by us. Signed in or not, photos you take and the pills you look up are never collected.

Who receives it

  • Google LLC: stores account data (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore, United States), hosts the official pill photos and keeps the request logs (Firebase Hosting), all on our behalf (a processor), and provides our email service, which stores emails you send us.

We have no affiliates. We don’t share consumer health data with anyone else, except if required by law. We don’t sell it.

Your rights

You can:

  • confirm whether we collect, share or sell your consumer health data, and get a list of who received it;
  • get a copy of it;
  • have it deleted, including from the companies above;
  • withdraw consent;
  • appeal if we refuse a request.

To make a request, email annirdev@gmail.com. We answer within 45 days (we may extend this once by 45 more days if needed, and will tell you). To appeal a decision, reply to our answer with “Appeal” in the subject; we answer appeals within 45 days. In Washington, if your appeal is denied, you can contact the Washington State Attorney General.

You can also do much of this yourself: delete medicines or people in the app, or delete the app to remove what is on your phone. If you signed in, Me › Account › Delete account deletes your account and its data and withdraws your consent.
