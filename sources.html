Pill and drug data

The app’s pill database is built from drug labels (Structured Product Labeling) published on DailyMed by the U.S. National Library of Medicine (NLM). The current version has 30,103 pills, from labels downloaded on September 30, 2026. We refresh it in app updates and keep discontinued pills, because old pills still turn up at home.

Courtesy of the National Library of Medicine. Pillmark is not affiliated with, reviewed or endorsed by the NLM, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the U.S. government.

Corrections and removal requests

If you own the rights to a photo and want it removed or credited differently, or if you find wrong pill information, email annirdev@gmail.com with the pill’s imprint and name. We aim to reply within 5 business days. Photo removals and data fixes take effect in the next app update, and removed photos are taken off our image storage right away.

Emergency services

Poison Help (number at the bottom of this page) is the national toll-free line for U.S. poison centers, coordinated by America’s Poison Centers. 911 is the U.S. emergency number. Pillmark only dials these numbers; it has no connection to these services.
