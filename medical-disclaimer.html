Medical Disclaimer

Last updated: October 18, 2026

Emergency? Call 911 for trouble breathing, someone who won’t wake up, or a seizure. For a possible poisoning or overdose, call Poison Help (number below): free, confidential, 24/7. If an opioid overdose is possible and naloxone (Narcan) is at hand, give it and call 911.

Not medical advice

Pilldex gives general information to help you organize medicines. It is not medical advice, and it doesn’t replace your doctor or pharmacist. It is not a medical device and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. Never start, stop or change a medicine or a dose because of something in the app. Using the app doesn’t create a doctor–patient or pharmacist–patient relationship.

Pill identification

  • The app compares the imprint, color and shape you give it with U.S. drug label data. A match does not prove what a pill is. Counterfeit pills can look exactly like real ones, and fake pills made to look like opioids, anti-anxiety medicines or stimulants may contain fentanyl.
  • Several different pills can share an imprint. Always compare every match, the size and any score line.
  • Pills from other countries, many supplements, compounded medicines, discontinued products, and damaged or worn pills may be missing or matched wrongly.
  • Official photos can differ from your pill (lighting, a newer version, another maker).
  • Never take a pill you identified only with an app. Ask a pharmacist; they can check it for free.

Reading pills with the camera

Photo reading helps you type the imprint. It can misread characters (for example 0 and O, 1 and I, 5 and S) or miss pressed-in imprints. Always check the imprint yourself against the pill.

Pill counter

The counter gives an estimate. It can miss pills that touch, overlap or blend into the surface, and it can count other objects. Check every count by hand before relying on it, especially for controlled substances, refills or anything you give to someone else. It is not intended for dispensing.

Reminders and your schedule

Reminders only work if notifications are allowed and your phone is on. A Focus mode, low-power settings or an iOS update can delay or silence them. Keep your own routine, especially for medicines where a missed or extra dose is dangerous. Your schedule in the app is what you entered: check it against your prescription label.

Drug information

Drug details come from labels published on DailyMed by the U.S. National Library of Medicine. They may not be the newest version and don’t cover every product. Read the label and leaflet that come with your medicine, and ask your pharmacist if anything differs.
